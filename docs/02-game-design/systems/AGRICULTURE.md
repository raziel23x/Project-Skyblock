# Agriculture System

## Purpose

Agriculture turns fragile survival into reliable food, renewable materials, and planned land use. It should reward understanding of soil, water, nutrients, crop choice, and environmental conditions.

## Core Design Rules

- Vanilla farmland and crop growth remain valid.
- Better systems provide control, efficiency, and resilience rather than mandatory replacement.
- Crop progression should emphasize cultivation, not random seed grinding.
- Food security should be achieved before industrial crop output.

## Progression

### Primitive Cultivation

- Hand-tilled soil
- Rain or carried water
- Basic seeds
- Compost and bone meal

### Managed Farming

- Irrigation
- Crop rotation or simplified nutrient balancing
- Mulching
- Greenhouses
- Seed selection

### Controlled Agriculture

- Nutrient solutions
- Hydroponics
- Environmental control
- Automated harvesting
- Seed stock management

### Advanced Agriculture

- Trait selection
- Specialized industrial crops
- Closed-loop water and nutrient recovery
- Cross-dimensional cultivation where sensible

## Inputs

- Seeds and starter specimens
- Soil or growth substrate
- Water
- Nutrients
- Light
- Heat
- Carbon source where appropriate
- Pollination where appropriate

## Outputs

- Food
- Seeds
- Fibers
- Oils
- Sugars and starches
- Dyes
- Medicines or reagents
- Biomass

## Manual Processes

- Planting
- Watering
- Soil preparation
- Compost application
- Harvesting
- Seed preservation
- Pest or disease recovery if included

## Automation

Automation may include planting, irrigation, harvesting, sorting, and nutrient dosing. Fully automated farming should require the player to understand crop requirements first.

## Vanilla Integration

- Vanilla crops grow normally.
- Redstone and villager crop farms remain possible.
- Bees retain their vanilla pollination benefits.
- Project greenhouses and hydroponics offer compactness, control, and specialized outputs.
- Existing modded crops should participate through tags and datapack definitions.

## Failure and Recovery

Agriculture should fail gradually rather than catastrophically where practical.

Possible failure states:

- Slow growth
- Reduced yield
- Nutrient depletion
- Poor seed quality
- Dry substrate

Players should be able to diagnose and recover without losing irreplaceable specimens.

## Restrictions

- Avoid crop tiers that are merely ore plants in disguise.
- Avoid making every crop require a separate machine.
- Avoid excessive micromanagement of minor variables.
- Rare crops should have a logical origin or bootstrap path.

## Open Questions

- Should seasons be supported but optional?
- Will soil nutrients be per-block, per-area, or represented through soil types?
- How should vanilla farmland remain competitive in early and midgame?
- Which crops become industrial feedstocks?

---

## Feature Review Checklist

Before implementation, confirm that the proposed feature:

- complements vanilla Minecraft rather than disabling it;
- has an understandable cause and effect;
- introduces knowledge before automation;
- supports the journey from survival to civilization;
- avoids unnecessary grind and arbitrary recipe chains;
- can be expressed through data where practical;
- has a clear place in progression;
- defines failure, recovery, and renewable paths;
- does not accidentally invalidate several other systems.

## Status

Design specification. Exact values, recipes, machine costs, and timings remain subject to playtesting.
